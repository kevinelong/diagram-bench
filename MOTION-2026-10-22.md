# Vector motion from our own SVGs (2026-10-22)

Z-Image cannot make video - it is a still-image diffusion model with no temporal dimension, and no
video model is installed. None of the clips below needed one: they animate the **structure** of SVGs
we already own, so every frame is drawn at its own resolution instead of being a zoomed bitmap.

Measured on the RTX 5070 (12 GB), headless Chromium at 1.25x DPI, ffmpeg 6.1.1 in WSL.

Each is written both as `.mp4` (h264, crf 18) and as a `.gif` at 460 px for quick viewing.

## Tools

`svg_motion.py` - element animation. Takes an SVG, walks its `rect`/`line`/`text` elements in document
order, and writes one frame per time step. Two effects:

    python svg_motion.py assemble grounded_ref/flow-1.svg out/assemble 48
    python svg_motion.py reveal   grounded_ref/flow-1.svg out/reveal   48

`assemble` slides each box up 70 units while fading it in, staggered by 0.12 of the timeline; an arrow
starts drawing once its boxes have settled; labels fade in last. `reveal` holds everything still and
animates `stroke-dashoffset` from the element's full length to zero, staggered across boxes then arrows.

`svg_morph.py` - interpolation between two documents with **matched structure**:

    python svg_morph.py a.svg b.svg out/morph 48

It refuses to run if the two files do not have the same number of `rect`/`line`/`text` elements, because
that is the correspondence problem and there is no honest way around it: five boxes cannot morph into
seven. Where the words differ, labels crossfade instead of pretending glyphs move.

## Seed sweeping is not drift - measured, and the honest answer is no

The flipbook was built to show the latent wandering between neighbouring seeds. It does not, and the
measurement says so. 24 tiles, `flat vector icon of an animal, simple shapes, minimal, white background`,
512 px, 4 steps, seeds 9000-9023, mean absolute pixel difference between pairs:

| seed distance | mean difference |
|---|---|
| 1 (neighbour) | 30.74 |
| 2 | 32.13 |
| 8 | 33.18 |
| far across the sweep | 30.41 |

If seeds formed a path, distance 1 would sit well below distance 8. They are within 8 % of each other, so
**adjacent seeds are already as different as seeds 23 apart**: the seed is an independent draw, not a step.
Visually the same holds - the sheet keeps one side-profile composition for all 24 tiles and the seed moves
the *palette* (grey, brown, black, orange) and small body details, not the layout.

`seed_flipbook.py` in this directory generates the sweep and writes `drift.json` with those four numbers:

    python seed_flipbook.py out/drift --count 24 --first 9000

Consequence for animation: a seed flipbook flickers, it does not move. Smooth motion between two images
needs interpolation - spherical interpolation of the initial noise, or an img2img chain at low denoise -
not seed + 1. Same lesson as the earlier prompt-variety test, from the other direction: a specific prompt
pins composition so hard that the seed only has colour and detail left to choose.

## Two gotchas that cost a render each

**SVG markers ignore `stroke-dashoffset`.** An arrowhead declared with `marker-end` renders at the line's
end point even when the dash is fully offset, so a "draw yourself" animation starts with arrowheads
floating in empty space. The fix is to hold `marker-end` back until the line is actually drawing:
frame 1 has zero markers, frame 24 has two (the arrows mid-draw), frame 48 has three.

**An SVG with no background rect renders on black.** Chromium's page background is what you see outside
the SVG canvas, so a document that only declares its elements gives a black frame. `grounded_ref` carries
a white `<rect>`; `svg/qwen3-coder_30b` does not, so renders need the wrapper to set `background:#fff`.

## Choosing the morph pair

`flow-1-s0.svg` and `flow-1-s1.svg` look like a good pair (same prompt, same element counts) but are
**geometrically identical** - the sampler produced the same layout twice, so the morph was invisible.
The pair that actually demonstrates the technique is the reference diagram against the model's rendering
of it: boxes at y=100/320/540/760, 240x110, slide to y=50/200/350/500, 224x80, arrows shorten to match.
At the midpoint (frame 24) the boxes sit at y=75.8/261.9/448.0/634.2 - exactly between the two.

## Why a video model is not the next step

Sizes are the actual weight files on Hugging Face, not guesses; free space is C: 72.4 GiB, D: 191.4 GiB,
so disk is not the constraint - 12 GB of VRAM is.

| model | weight size | verdict on 12 GB |
|---|---|---|
| LTX-Video 2B v0.9.5 | **5.91 GiB**, one file | the only cheap entry point; text-to-video at small resolution |
| Wan 2.2 TI2V 5B | **9.31 GiB** plus a text encoder | tight; needs offload, slow, and no better for diagrams |
| SVD-XT | 8.90 GiB | image-to-video only, no prompt - wrong tool for diagrams |

For what we actually make - diagrams, icons, layouts - animating the SVG beats any video model: the
result is resolution-independent, deterministic, seconds to render, and the text stays typographically
correct. A video model would earn its 6 GiB only for real-world footage, which is a separate decision.
