THE BLOCKER IT NAMED, AND WHAT IT ACTUALLY WAS
`cab873f` says: "its 1:1 binding check fails through event_generate for a reason not yet diagnosed - possibly the
synthetic press not establishing the grab". **It was not the grab, and the grab was never missing.** Measured today, on
master, from the same block:

    the strip|pane sash widens the strip by the drag and narrows the pane by it   FAIL ((618, 618, 617, 617), (756, 480, 617, 617))
    a press and a motion through the binding move the boundary 1:1               FAIL 480
    and the pane canvas follows it                                               FAIL (480, 618)

The even split at a 2560 px window is 618 px for the pane, a +40 px drag should give 578, and the answer is **480** -
`CONTENTS_W_MAX`. The motion *was* delivered and *was* applied (the pane canvas really moved to 480); what moved it to
the wrong number is one stale clamp left behind by the 2026-11-02 decision to remove the caps:

    _sash_apply, strip|pane:   low = max(floors[COL_STRIP], total - CONTENTS_W_MAX)

`_column_caps()` had already been emptied (`10 ** 6` everywhere, "no cap where the reader is steering") and
`_even_widths` honoured that, but the drag path still capped the pane at the constant - so the first motion of every
drag on the strip|pane boundary snapped the pane to 480 and then dragged 1:1 from there. A press-and-drag through the
binding is exactly what shows it; a handler-level drag (`drag()` in the suite) hides it, because it asks for the same
boundary 40 px from a *different* starting width. The suite's own detail line printed the 480 and nobody had read it as
arithmetic.

WHAT THE FOUR PROPERTIES ARE NOW, EACH WITH ITS TEST

**1. Explicit pointer capture on press, every move guarded.** The branch did nothing here (`Sash` was untouched); master
already took `grab_set()` on the press and `grab_release()` on the release, but nothing ever *checked* the capture, and
`_motion` only asked whether a press had happened. Added: `_press` records `_captured` (did the grab actually get
taken?), `_motion` is guarded by `_has_capture()` - `grab_current() is self` when a grab was taken, and permissive when
it was not, so a window that cannot be grabbed cannot strand a drag - and `_release` clears it. The press also stopped
being "a drag of 0 px": it announces with `on_press` -> `App._sash_begin`, which records the starting widths and says
the *first motion* applies immediately (see 2).

    the press takes the pointer explicitly, so the moves are known to be this drag's  PASS (<Sash .!frame2.!frame4.!sash>, True)
    and a move with the capture gone moves nothing                                  PASS (618, 618)
    and the release gives the capture back                                         PASS (None, False)

The middle one is the guard measured: with the grab released mid-drag, a `<B1-Motion>` of +40 px delivered through the
binding moves the boundary by 0 px. (This suite can only synthesise events, so the grab is handled deliberately - the
press takes it for real, and one check gives it away and puts it back.)

**2. Per-frame coalescing, the first motion immediate, the release flushing.** The branch had this; it was measured but
never asserted, and the assertion that existed allowed it (`counts['layout'] <= 41`, "once per motion plus once per
commit"). What was wrong is that the *press* spent the "apply immediately" flag by calling `on_drag(0)`, so the first
real motion - the one that has to hold 1:1 - went into the next frame. Measured through the binding before the fix:
`a press and a motion through the binding move the boundary 1:1 FAIL 618` (nothing moved until the release flushed the
target into the canvas, `(578, 618)`). After `_sash_begin`/first-motion the same block reads:

    a press and a motion through the binding move the boundary 1:1   PASS (618 -> 578)
    and the pane canvas follows it                                   PASS (578, 618)

and the coalescing claim, measured on one block with both numbers from the same instrument:

    40 motions in, at most 4 relayouts out - one per frame, not one per pixel event  PASS {'refresh': 1, 'tiles': 0,
                     'resize': 3, 'layout': 3, 'motions': 40}

      before (0.29.1 baseline and 0.30.1 master, same block, same 40 motions): layout 41
      after  (0.31.0):                                                        layout  3   (-93 %)

and the drag is proved to have happened in that block (`and the drag did move the boundary ... PASS (618, 738)`) so the
count cannot be low for the wrong reason. The interiors (title reflow, prompt wrap, window minimum) are the deferred
half; the *grid* moves every frame, which is the split the branch introduced and the gallery suite keeps honest.

**3. A hit area that follows the display, tested at explicit positions.** Added `sash_px(root)` - `SASH_W` (8 px) at
96 dpi, scaled by `tk scaling`, 12 px at 144 dpi - and wired it through one number, `App._sash_w`, read at start and
re-read by `_apply_sash_scale()`: the three sash widgets, the two root grid minsizes, `_layout_chrome` (three of them
plus the pads and the pane's bar) and `_pane_total_w`. This window's geometry is otherwise pixels-by-design (`ACTION_PAD`
says so, and that comment now names this one exception: a boundary is a pointer target, not a legible line).

    the hit area is SASH_W at 96 dpi and scales with the display                 PASS (8, 12, 8)
    and the sash on screen is that wide, and so is the grid column it sits in    PASS (8, 8, 8)
    and the layout pays for three of them in its chrome                         PASS (90, 8)
    every pixel of the hit area belongs to the sash, and neither neighbour takes one
        PASS (['.!sash', '.!sash.!frame', '.!sash'], ['.!frame2', '.!frame2.!frame4.!canvas.!frame'])
    re-reading the display puts the same width back, and the layout agrees       PASS (8, 8)

The fourth one is the "tested around explicit boundary positions rather than the drawn line" half: `winfo_containing`
at the left edge, the middle and the last pixel of the hit area returns the sash or its 2 px grip, and one pixel either
side returns the neighbour. The grip is *not* the target; the 8 px is.

**4. Home/End/arrows as the keyboard contract, `preventDefault` included.** `Sash` already bound the four keys and
returned `'break'`, but the suite drove `app._sash_step(...)`/`_sash_jump(...)` directly - a function call, not a key.
Added: the keys are driven through the binding now, and the `'break'` is asserted as well, so a window-level binding
cannot also act.

WHAT WAS A TEST'S FAULT, NOT THE APP'S - AND WHAT THAT FIX CHANGED
Twelve of the 22 are the four-column screen, not the sash: the app opens on the folders screen (2026-11-03), where the
pane, the prompt column and two of the three sashes are `grid_remove`d on purpose, and the strip is the folder list. A
block that measures those columns was measuring removed widgets. That is the same family B3 needed fixed for and the
same fix - `on_screen(root, app, mode)`, the screen switch a click makes, then a check that names what is on screen -
applied to the three blocks that need it (the layout block, and the finished-render block at the end of the file).
No assertion was weakened, removed or reordered: the same checks run, against a window that is showing what they
describe. Measured before the switch, per block: the render block's `_tile_widgets` was `[]` with the file in `_files`
(probe: `%TEMP%\tkprobe\render_probe.py`), and with `set_view_mode('columns')` the same two events built four tile
widgets.

Two more were genuinely stale expectations of an *older* decision that the columns screen had been hiding:

- `the columns and the tile are what the rule asks for` - the tile is a *rung* of the ladder since 2026-11-01
  (`tile_bucket`, round down, `TILE_STEP = 16`), and the check compared it to the band value with a tolerance of
  `TILE_CHROME` (10). At a 2524 px strip the two agreed by luck; at a 604 px strip the rung is 128 and the band value
  141, i.e. 13 px apart. The check now states the ladder contract: the same column count, the tile is a rung, and the
  band value is above it and below the next rung.

THE RE-PINS - EACH ONE A DECISION THAT CHANGED, AND WHAT THE OLD LINE ASSERTED
Every one of these is marked `RE-PINNED 2026-11-14` in `test_sizing.py` with the same sentence.

1. `and the pane is the one at its own cap, which is stated rather than accidental` -> **and no column is held by a cap:
   the pane takes the same share as the other three.** Decision: 2026-11-02 removed the caps ("no cap where the reader
   is steering"). Old expectation: `even_2560[COL_PANE] == CONTENTS_W_MAX` (480). At 2560 px the even split is
   618/618/617/617, so the old line could only ever fail once the caps were gone.
2. `and the strip is then wide enough for a 206 px tile` -> **and the strip's share at 2560 px is still enough for the
   nine at the fit size.** Same decision: the 206 px tile was what the *capped* pane left over to the strip (756 px of
   strip). With an even share the strip has 618 px and its tile is 191 - the rule holds (>= the 160 px fit size, inside
   the 320 cap), the old number is history, and the number is still printed in the detail line.
3. `strip|pane stops with the pane at its stated minimum` -> **... at its floor, which the policy says is nothing**.
   Decision: 2026-11-02 gave the strip and the pane a floor of 0. Old expectation: `_contents_w == CONTENTS_W_MIN`
   (180).
4. `Left/Right move the strip|pane boundary 16 px each way` -> the same three numbers around the *floor* instead of
   around 180: `(0, 16, 0)` rather than `(180, 196, 180)`. Same decision.
5. `Shift moves it 64, or to the end of its range when that is nearer` -> `min(limit, floor + 4 * SASH_STEP)` rather
   than `min(limit, CONTENTS_W_MIN + 64)`. Same decision.
6. `and at the other end the gallery column is its two floors and the prompt has the rest` -> "its two floors" is now
   `0 + 0`. Old expectation: `_contents_w == CONTENTS_W_MIN`. Same decision.
7. `the other end is the pane's widest, which never takes the strip below its floor` -> the strip's floor is `0`, so
   the check compares against `_column_floors()[COL_STRIP]` instead of the pre-2026-11-02 constant `GAL_FLOOR_W` (510).
   This one is a *fix of a fix*: it passed on master only because `CONTENTS_W_MAX` was still capping the pane, i.e. the
   stale clamp was holding a stale expectation up.
8. `so a window that opens on that file starts with a layout the reader set` -> presence, not truthiness
   (`_px_setting(...) is not None`). Same decision: 0 is a width the reader set. Old expectation: `all(...)` over the
   values, which reads 0 as "never seen".
9. `the layout is applied once per motion plus once per commit - not once per pixel event` -> **40 motions in, at most 4
   relayouts out - one per frame, not one per pixel event**, plus a new check that the drag really moved. Old
   expectation: `<= 41`. This one got *tighter*, not looser.

APP CHANGES (all in `zimage_ui.py`)
- the pane's ceiling is `_column_caps()[COL_PANE]`, not `CONTENTS_W_MAX`, in all four places a width is clamped
  (`_sash_apply`, `_set_contents_w`, `_contents_w_limit`, `_share_gallery`), and `__init__` no longer clamps the
  remembered pane width into `[CONTENTS_W_MIN, CONTENTS_W_MAX]` - that clamp is what made a remembered 0 into 180;
- `_px_setting` returns 0 for a remembered 0 (and `None` only when the key is absent), and `_layout_user` asks
  `is not None`;
- `sash_px()` + `App._sash_w` + `_apply_sash_scale()` (the scaled hit area, everywhere it is counted);
- `Sash._press` takes the grab explicitly, records `_captured`, and announces with `on_press`; `Sash._has_capture` +
  the guarded `_motion`; `_release` clears it;
- `App._sash_begin` + the first *motion* applying immediately (was the press);
- comments/docstrings that had gone stale with the caps and the floors: the pane-width block at the constants, the
  `Sash` docstring, `_layout_chrome`, `ACTION_PAD_X`'s "not scaled by the display" (now names the one exception).
