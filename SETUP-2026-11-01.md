describes. Everything below was executed and measured on this machine; nothing is copied from another host's numbers
unless it says *(README)*.

## Measured performance (this card, 1024 px, cfg 1.0, `--cache-none`)

| Profile | steps | wall | VRAM peak | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `z-image turbo` (nvfp4) | 4 | 159.0 s | — | first ever run, includes weight load |
| `z-image turbo` (nvfp4) | 9 | 140.4 s | 7,201 MiB of 8,188 | second run, model already on disk |
| `z-image turbo` (nvfp4) | 9 | 144.6 s | 7,158 MiB | after the torch cu130 upgrade |
| `z-image turbo int8` | 9 | 140.4 s | 7,206 MiB | int8_convrot + fp8_mixed encoder — **no faster** |

Where the 140 s goes (server log): **65 s "Model Initialization"**, ~15 s of sampling for 9 steps, the rest text encode
and VAE decode. The 65 s is re-paid on **every prompt** because `--cache-none` disables the model cache — it is the
staged/dynamic-VRAM load, not the fp4 dequant specifically, which is why the int8 weights changed nothing.

The README's 10.6 s at 1024 px was measured on a 12 GB card that can keep the model resident. **On 8,188 MiB the model
must be staged per prompt**; peak with cache-none is already 7.2 GiB, so a warm cache is the open question recorded at
the bottom of this file.

## Deviations from the README, and why

1. **`DEFAULT_MODEL` changed** in `zimage_gen.py` from `'flux2 klein 9b uncensored'` to `'z-image turbo'`. The 9B's
   weights are absent and its *(README)* 8,837 MiB peak at 512 px exceeds this card's 8,188 MiB total. Opening on a
   profile whose files are missing makes the first Generate fail with `value_not_in_list`.
2. **`leejet/Z-Image-Turbo-GGUF` does not load in ComfyUI.** It is packaged for stable-diffusion.cpp — the server logs
   `compatibility mode 'sd.cpp' [arch:lumina2]` and then dies with
   `size mismatch for x_pad_token: [3840] from checkpoint vs [1, 3840] in current model` (same for `cap_pad_token`).
   `gguf-org/z-image-gguf` ships a ComfyUI workflow, but for a different node pack (`LoaderGGUF`/`VaeGGUF` from
   calcuis/comfy), not city96's `UnetLoaderGGUF`.
3. **torch cu130 was installed** because the server warned *"You need pytorch with cu130 or higher to use optimized CUDA
   operations"*. It flipped `comfy_kitchen backend cuda` from `disabled: True` to `disabled: False`, but generation time
   did not change (144.6 s vs 140.4 s) — the emulated-nvfp4 path is not what the kitchen backend accelerates.
4. **The 4 GiB GGUF and its encoder are still on disk** but unused. They cost nothing to keep and are the fallback if a
   ComfyUI-native Z-Image GGUF is ever published.

Nothing here is a Windows service; all of it dies on restart. Work down this list.

**1. Bring the app up and prove the loop closes (2 min).**

**2. VRAM gate before every session.**

**3. Upscale: the one cheap functional win (64 MB).**

```
  https://github.com/xinntao/Real-ESRGAN/releases/download/v0.1.0/RealESRGAN_x4plus.pth
```

## Warm cache: tried, rejected

The server was restarted **without** `--cache-none` and given a 512 px render. It was still running after ~7 minutes,
holding 6,998 MiB with the GPU at 55 % — where a 1024 px render costs 140 s end to end with `--cache-none`. Keeping the
model resident pushes the card to the edge of its 8,188 MiB and the staging/offload churn costs more than the cache
saves. Killed, and the served config is `--cache-none` again. A 512 px render with `--cache-none` was not separately
timed; expect it under the 140 s figure, and treat 140 s per 1024 px image as the honest cost of this card.
